How to play Sudoku

Sudoku is a logic puzzle played on a 9×9 grid. The grid is split into nine 3×3 boxes, and a handful of cells already contain digits. Your job is to fill every remaining cell so that each row, each column and each box holds the digits 1 to 9 exactly once.

The rules in three lines

  1. Every row must contain 1–9 with no repeats.
  2. Every column must contain 1–9 with no repeats.
  3. Every 3×3 box must contain 1–9 with no repeats.

That is the whole rule set. There is no arithmetic and no guessing: a well-formed puzzle has one unique solution that can be reached by pure deduction.

Playing in this app

Tap or click a cell, then pick a digit from the number row or press it on your keyboard. Turn on Notes to pencil in candidate digits; when you place a digit correctly it is removed from the notes in that row, column and box. Undo steps back through your moves, and each round gives you three hints. Three wrong entries end the round, so wrong digits are worth undoing rather than leaving in place.

What the four levels mean

Difficulty here is set by how many clues you start with: Easy begins with 45 filled cells, Medium with 36, Hard with 30 and Expert with 26. Fewer clues means fewer obvious placements, so you lean more on notes and on the techniques below.

Techniques, easiest first

1. Naked single

A cell where eight digits are already excluded by its row, column and box. Only one candidate remains, so it must go there. This is the workhorse of Easy and Medium.

2. Hidden single

A digit that can only fit in one cell of a given row, column or box, even if that cell has several candidates. Scan each box for a digit that appears in just one place and place it.

3. Naked pair

Two cells in the same row, column or box that both hold exactly the same two candidates. Those two digits must occupy those two cells, so remove them from every other cell in that unit.

4. Pointing pair

Inside a box, a candidate digit restricted to one row or column. The digit must sit in that line within the box, which eliminates it from the rest of that row or column.

5. Cross-hatching

For a digit, sweep its existing placements across the grid and see where it is still allowed in each box. Often three boxes line up and force the position in the fourth.

Good habits

Work in passes: place every obvious digit first, then fill notes everywhere, then look for patterns. Keep notes tidy rather than guessing and erasing, and if a puzzle stalls, switch to a different digit or a different box instead of staring at the same cell.

Frequently asked questions

Is the game free?

Yes. No account, no ads, no downloads. It runs in your browser.

Does every puzzle have exactly one solution?

Yes. Puzzles are built by removing clues only while a single solution remains, so a logical path to the answer always exists.

How many mistakes can I make?

Three wrong entries end the round. Wrong digits show in red, and you can undo them.

Is my progress saved?

Yes. The puzzle, timer and notes are stored in your browser, so you can close the tab and continue later on the same device.
